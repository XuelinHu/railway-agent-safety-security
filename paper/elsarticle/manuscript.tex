\title{Provenance-Preserving Evidence-Gated Knowledge-Graph Augmentation for Low-Resource Safety Information Extraction}

% JSSR uses double-blind review. Upload title-page.tex separately.
\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{abstract}
Safety reports contain critical evidence about hazards, failures, controls,
events, and causal relations. Low-resource bilingual extraction, however, is
vulnerable to split leakage, uncontrolled graph priors, unsupported relations,
and untraceable outputs.

This study proposes a provenance-preserving, evidence-gated knowledge-graph
augmentation framework. A graph built only from permitted training data
supplies candidate priors, while the current report remains authoritative. The
framework combines compact 4-bit quantized low-rank adaptation,
evidence-preserving windows, provenance-isolated context that excludes
candidates supported only by the current document, deterministic span
reconstruction, conservative entity selection, and relation verification.

On the frozen validation split under duplicate-cluster-isolated document-level
partitioning, graph augmentation expands candidate coverage, entity selection
suppresses over-generation, and verification filters relations that fail local
evidence or ontology rules. Staged generation, evidence binding, and selection
improve the precision--recall balance, traceability, and auditability of the
resulting risk graph.

By treating graph knowledge as an evidence-constrained prior, the framework
offers controlled augmentation, traceable outputs, and auditable decisions. It
is designed for auditable low-resource bilingual workflows, including settings
with only a small reviewed corpus, and can assist analysts with risk-element
identification, relation screening, evidence tracing, risk-graph construction,
and human-review prioritization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Accident investigation reports and safety-management documents record how
hazards, technical failures, human and organizational factors, barriers, and
consequences interact. Converting these reports into typed risk graphs can
support retrieval, trend analysis, and expert review. The task is more
demanding than ordinary named-entity recognition: a useful output must preserve
mention boundaries, relation direction, claim status, and the exact evidence
from which each assertion was derived. Errors are also asymmetric. Missing a
candidate reduces recall, whereas inserting an unsupported causal edge may
distort a later safety analysis.

Three practical constraints make this problem difficult. First, expert review
is costly, so the amount of trusted domain annotation is small relative to the
linguistic and operational variety of safety reports. Second, the corpus is
heterogeneous. English railway investigations use detailed narrative evidence,
whereas Chinese emergency and safety-management documents often follow
recurrent administrative templates. Third, generative models can return fluent
but unsupported claims or structurally invalid output, a known concern in
natural-language generation~\cite{ji2023hallucination}. Conventional entity or
relation F1 alone does not reveal whether an extracted edge has source evidence
or satisfies the domain ontology.

Knowledge graphs (KGs) offer structured priors over entity types and
relations~\cite{hogan2021kg}, and retrieval-augmented generation can expose
relevant external context to a language model~\cite{lewis2020rag}. However,
generic KG
augmentation is unsafe for the present setting. A graph may leak information
across evaluation splits, reproduce a label from the document currently being
used for training, or encourage a plausible edge that the source does not
state. The central design question is therefore not simply whether a KG is
available, but how graph context can be used without displacing source evidence
as the authority.

We therefore propose a staged, provenance-preserving and evidence-gated
knowledge-graph augmentation framework for bilingual safety extraction. Its
design is motivated not only by limited annotation, but also by uneven
computing availability across organizations, regions, and countries. In many
safety-analysis settings, large-scale training infrastructure and repeated
model tuning are impractical. We therefore examine how bounded graph context,
compact adaptation, and deterministic gates can improve the usefulness and
auditability of extraction under constrained data and hardware resources.

The contributions of this study are as follows:

\begin{itemize}
  \item We develop a resource-aware bilingual safety-extraction framework that
  governs the combination of multilingual representation and compact
  adaptation. XLM-R supplies general cross-lingual modeling and QLoRA makes
  local fine-tuning feasible~\cite{conneau2020xlmr,dettmers2023qlora}, but
  neither alone specifies how graph priors, source evidence, and constrained
  hardware should be jointly governed in a safety workflow.
  \item We introduce provenance-isolated KG context and hierarchical evidence
  gates. Whereas generic KGs and retrieval augmentation can supply candidate
  context~\cite{hogan2021kg,lewis2020rag}, our design excludes candidates
  supported only by the current document, keeps source evidence authoritative,
  and applies separate entity-acceptance and relation-compliance gates before
  retaining graph objects.
  \item We provide document-level de-duplicated validation across D10, D25,
  and completed D100 runs, comparing SOE, EAE, HRGE, EVGE, CFE, and PGE with
  additional encoder and classifier baselines. This design addresses repeated
  voting from overlapping windows, a known concern in span-based extraction
  evaluation~\cite{li2022ner}; in matched D100 validation, PGE has the highest
  relation F1 and ties CFE for the highest entity F1.
\end{itemize}

The contribution of this work lies in the governed composition of these
components for low-resource bilingual safety extraction: graph knowledge is
restricted to a traceable candidate prior, source evidence remains decisive,
and every retained graph object has an auditable acceptance path. The resulting
design improves the quality of the retained graph while preserving an explicit
boundary between automated extraction and expert safety judgement.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Safety information extraction}

Information extraction commonly decomposes text into entity recognition and
relation classification. Neural approaches have improved multilingual named
entity recognition, but boundary errors, repeated mentions, and domain shift
remain important evaluation issues~\cite{li2022ner}. Safety reports add a
strong provenance requirement: an entity or relation is useful only when an
analyst can inspect the source span and distinguish an explicit observation
from an inferred, uncertain, or normative statement. Much accident-report
analysis focuses on event categories, causes, or recommendations, whereas the
present task requires a single typed representation spanning events, hazards,
failures, actors, assets, barriers, mitigations, and their evidence.

Prompt-driven graph construction has also been explored directly in accident
reports. Liu and Luo~\cite{liu2025llmaccidentkg} combine a revised Accimap
ontology with chained prompts, chain-of-thought prompting, and
self-consistency to construct a causal graph from 282 fall-from-height reports.
Their best reported scores are 0.94 entity F1 and 0.90 relation precision;
however, relation recall and relation F1 are not reported, and the evaluation
uses a narrow eight-entity-class, six-relation schema. We therefore treat this
work as a relevant workflow precedent rather than a directly comparable
accuracy baseline.

\subsection{Knowledge graphs and retrieval augmentation}

Knowledge graphs provide explicit entities, relations, and constraints that
can complement distributional language models~\cite{hogan2021kg}. Retrieval
augmentation can reduce the need to encode all domain knowledge in model
parameters and can expose provenance-bearing context at generation time
~\cite{lewis2020rag}. Yet a retrieved graph edge is not evidence that the edge
holds in the current report. Our work therefore distinguishes a \emph{graph
prior} from \emph{source authority}: graph context may nominate a type or
relation, but acceptance is conditioned on text-local evidence and a frozen
ontology.

This distinction is consistent with evidence-integration practice in other
high-stakes scientific domains. KnetMiner organizes heterogeneous evidence for
gene discovery and complex-trait analysis~\cite{hassanipak2021knetminer}, while
crop knowledge-graph systems have used typed multi-source structure for
polyphenotype gene mining~\cite{zhang2024croppolyphenotypekg}. More recently,
cross-residual KG learning has been studied for robust multi-trait gene--trait
prioritization in rice~\cite{wang2026crossresidual}. These studies motivate
domain-specific graph representation and evidence tracing, but their prediction
target is biological prioritization rather than source-grounded safety
information extraction. We use them as application precedents, not as direct
accuracy baselines.

\subsection{Grounded generation and selective verification}

Hallucination research shows that well-formed generated text can remain
inconsistent with its source~\cite{ji2023hallucination}. Schema-constrained
generation reduces malformed structures but does not by itself guarantee
factual support. GenIE~\cite{josifoski2022genie}, for example, couples an
autoregressive encoder--decoder with global triplet-structure constraints and
entity/relation prefix tries. It reaches 91.48 micro-F1 on the small Wiki-NRE
evaluation schema, but 68.93 micro-F1 and 30.46 macro-F1 on the much larger
REBEL schema, illustrating how candidate-space size and class imbalance affect
apparently high headline results. Its grounding is to a predefined KB
catalogue; our additional requirement is that every retained item be anchored
to evidence in the current report. Selective prediction offers a complementary principle: a
system may abstain when evidence is insufficient. We instantiate this
principle through a gold-independent gate and deterministic verification. This
design trades output coverage for a smaller graph whose structural and
evidence conditions can be audited.

\subsection{Low-resource multilingual adaptation}

Multilingual encoders such as XLM-RoBERTa (XLM-R) provide a strong
general-purpose basis for cross-lingual sequence
labeling~\cite{conneau2020xlmr}. Quantized low-rank
adaptation makes local fine-tuning of generative models feasible on limited
hardware~\cite{dettmers2023qlora}. The present study uses XLM-R in the
Multilingual BIO Encoder (MBE) comparison system and uses
Qwen3~\cite{qwen2025qwen3} in the trainable generative systems. The focus is the
interaction among reviewed-document budget, graph context, evidence
verification, and abstention rather than a new encoder or fine-tuning
algorithm.

\section{Problem formulation and corpus}
\label{sec:problem-corpus}

\subsection{Evidence-grounded risk extraction}

Let a document $d$ contain source segments $S_d$ with stable segment
identifiers and global character offsets. An entity mention is defined in
Equation~\eqref{eq:entity-mention}:
\begin{equation}
e_i=(b_i,u_i,y_i,q_i),
\label{eq:entity-mention}
\end{equation}
where $[b_i,u_i)$ is the global character span, $y_i$ is an ontology type, and
$q_i$ is source evidence containing the mention. Repeated textual mentions are
distinct whenever their spans differ. A relation is defined in
Equation~\eqref{eq:relation-object}:
\begin{equation}
r_j=(e_s,e_t,z_j,c_j,Q_j),
\label{eq:relation-object}
\end{equation}
where $e_s$ and $e_t$ are directed endpoints, $z_j$ is a relation type, $c_j$
is a claim status, and $Q_j$ is one or more evidence spans. The extraction
function returns a graph $G_d=(E_d,R_d)$ together with provenance, rather than
an ungrounded set of normalized names.

The ontology defines a set $\Sigma_z$ of legal source--target type signatures
for each relation $z$. The structural-admissibility condition is
\begin{equation}
(y_s,y_t)\in\Sigma_z.
\label{eq:signature-admissibility}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:signature-admissibility} is necessary but not
sufficient for semantic correctness. Consequently, we evaluate both extraction
accuracy against reviewed annotations and operational safety properties of the
predicted graph.

\subsection{Corpus sources and governance}

The source corpus comprises 1,458 successfully parsed public safety documents:
271 English reports from the UK Rail Accident Investigation Branch and 1,187
Chinese safety, emergency-management, and regulatory documents. A
near-duplicate-aware selection produced 150 reviewed documents and 370
reviewed text blocks. Table~\ref{tab:corpus}
summarizes the current benchmark inventory. Source text is retained or
redistributed only when its license permits; otherwise the release will contain
metadata, source links, hashes, processing code, and redistributable
annotations.

\subsection{Ontology and annotation protocol}

Ontology version 1.0 defines 15 entity types: event, hazard, asset, actor,
operation, failure, human factor, organizational factor, environment, barrier,
consequence, mitigation, location, time, and regulation. It also defines 15
directed relation types, legal endpoint signatures, and four claim statuses:
explicit, inferred, normative, and uncertain. Annotators select the shortest
complete mention, preserve repeated mentions, attach segment identifiers and
character offsets, and add a relation only when at least one source span
supports it.

Model suggestions are pre-annotations rather than ground truth. Human review
accepts, rejects, or edits every candidate. A stratified 20-document,
training-only subset has been prepared for two independent human reviews. The
independent agreement study is outside the present experimental snapshot and
is not reported as a completed result. Its frozen protocol covers exact entity
span, conditional entity type, conditional relation, evidence, claim status,
and adjudication statistics. No teacher model is treated as the final judge.

\section{Proposed method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Framework overview}

Figures~\ref{fig:pipeline}--\ref{fig:representation-flow} use complementary views
of the same implemented pipeline. To avoid repeated semantics,
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} is limited to system-level control flow,
Figure~\ref{fig:innovations} isolates acceptance and verification logic,
Figure~\ref{fig:kg-construction} isolates prompt-context construction, and
Figure~\ref{fig:representation-flow} isolates graph and embedding data
transformations. Together, these views show how provenance, source evidence,
and deterministic gates constrain the path from candidate generation to
retained risk-graph output. The subset KG is a train-only prompt prior, and the
current report remains the only source of evidence for retained output.

Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} gives the complete experimental architecture. To make
each system identifiable by function rather than by an arbitrary version
number, we use the names and abbreviations defined in Table~\ref{tab:systems}.
Phase A freezes reviewed documents, document-level splits, nested training
budgets, evidence-preserving windows, and ontology policy. Phase B constructs
three prompt conditions: Source-Only Extraction (SOE) receives no KG
instruction, Exact-Anchor Extraction (EAE) receives conservative exact hints,
and High-Recall Graph Extraction (HRGE) receives bounded high-recall graph
context. These generators are trained separately but use the same Qwen3-4B
snapshot, QLoRA configuration, compact target, and deterministic
source-expansion code in Phase C.

Phase D derives Evidence-Verified Graph Extraction (EVGE) by filtering HRGE
relations and applies the same conservative entity gate to HRGE candidates.
Conservative-Fusion Extraction (CFE) pairs the gated entities with raw HRGE
relations whose endpoints survive, whereas Provenance-Gated Extraction (PGE)
pairs the same entities with verified EVGE relations whose endpoints survive.
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} therefore corresponds to the six QLoRA-based rows in
Table~\ref{tab:systems}. The separately trained Multilingual BIO Encoder (MBE)
comparison is not part of this graph-augmentation architecture. EVGE, CFE, and
PGE are deterministic filters or compositions, not additional trained
generators.

Figure~\ref{fig:innovations} expands the conservative fusion and verification
portion of the architecture. HRGE and deterministic source expansion produce
entity candidates $E_{\mathrm{HRGE}}$ and relation candidates
$R_{\mathrm{HRGE}}$. A relation enters $R_{\mathrm{EVGE}}$ only if all
reference, non-self, ontology-signature, claim-status, evidence-format, and
local endpoint-co-occurrence checks pass. Independently, an HRGE entity enters
$E_{\mathrm{CFE}}$ when at least one of three gold-independent signals fires:
exact normalized text-and-type agreement with EAE, a same-type source-gated
HRGE anchor, or participation in an EVGE relation. Duplicate normalized
text--type candidates are collapsed. CFE combines $E_{\mathrm{CFE}}$ with
endpoint-filtered $R_{\mathrm{HRGE}}$; PGE combines the same entity set with
endpoint-filtered $R_{\mathrm{EVGE}}$. Failed checks and abstentions are
recorded rather than silently discarded.

Figure~\ref{fig:kg-construction} details only the KG context supplied to the EAE
and HRGE prompts; it is not a second extraction pipeline. For an example from
document $d$, Phase A constructs the provenance-bearing graph from reviewed
documents allowed by the current training budget and removes support whose only
provenance is $d$. Phase B matches the current source window and performs frozen
BGE-M3 retrieval. Phase C produces balanced exact hints for EAE and, for HRGE,
source-matched anchors with type purity at least 0.8, typed edge priors observed
outside $d$ whose endpoints occur in the source, and semantic relation patterns
with similarity at least 0.72. Phase D applies the frozen HRGE caps of 12
anchors (at most two per type), six edge priors, and four semantic patterns.
The EAE and HRGE contexts are used in separate QLoRA runs and are explicitly
presented as hints, not facts.

Figure~\ref{fig:representation-flow} makes the graph data structure and its
representation transformations explicit. Each training-subset record is a
directed typed triple with claim status, document and segment identifiers, and
supporting source spans. Let $\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$ contain the
$\ell_2$-normalized BGE-M3 embeddings of $m$ eligible relation-pattern records,
and let $\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ be the normalized embedding of the current
source window. Similarity scoring and bounded retrieval are defined in
Equation~\eqref{eq:embedding-retrieval}:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{s}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{q},\qquad
\mathcal{I}_k=\operatorname{TopK}(\mathbf{s};k).
\label{eq:embedding-retrieval}
\end{equation}
The selected indices identify candidate relation patterns rather than accepted
facts. The source and evidence gate retains only bounded prompt items that meet
the implemented anchor, endpoint, provenance, and local-source conditions.
PGE then emits a directed risk graph whose entities and verified relations
retain their evidence identifiers and claim status.

\subsection{Evidence-preserving semantic windowing}

Long and structurally noisy reports cannot be safely truncated at an arbitrary
token boundary. We construct windows preferentially at sentence, paragraph,
line, or whitespace boundaries, using an 800-character segment cap and
160-character overlap. Entity spans and relation evidence quotes are protected
from splitting. When the endpoints and evidence of a relation cannot co-occur
in an ordinary sequential window, a minimal relation-rescue window retains the
required source regions and records their noncontiguous provenance.

The window builder preserves entity spans and relation-evidence coverage while
bounding prompt length. Window counts, rescue-window statistics, and execution
checks are recorded in the released artifacts. The validation construction
covers all reviewed entity and relation objects used for evaluation.

\subsection{Leakage-controlled knowledge-graph context}

The subset KG is built exclusively from reviewed documents permitted by the
current training budget. Every concept, mention, relation, and source record
retains provenance. For a training example from document $d$, candidates whose
only supporting provenance is $d$ are excluded. This provenance-isolation rule
prevents document-only candidates from being supplied back to the prompt;
aggregate support statistics for candidates retained from other documents
remain those stored in the frozen subset graph.

Two graph contexts are studied. EAE receives a conservative set of balanced,
exactly matched concept hints. HRGE receives three bounded context types:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item source-matched entity anchors whose empirical type purity is at least
  0.8;
  \item typed edge priors observed outside the current document and whose
  endpoints co-occur in the current source; and
  \item semantically retrieved relation patterns from a frozen multilingual
  BGE-M3 encoder~\cite{chen2024bgem3}.
\end{enumerate}

HRGE is limited to 12 anchors, two anchors per type, six edge priors, and four
semantic patterns above a frozen similarity threshold of 0.72. The prompt
states that these items are hints rather than facts and that unsupported
entities or relations must not be emitted.

\subsection{Compact candidate generation}

The trainable variants share the same Qwen3-4B base snapshot, reviewed windows,
compact structured target, optimizer, and token limit. SOE sees no KG
instruction. EAE and HRGE differ only in their frozen context construction.
QLoRA uses rank 8, alpha 16, dropout 0.05, 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4) double
quantization, bfloat16 compute, one epoch, batch size 1, gradient accumulation
4, and learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$. AdamW uses the PyTorch defaults
($\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.999$, $\epsilon=10^{-8}$, and weight decay 0.01)
without warm-up or a learning-rate scheduler. Prompt tokens are masked from the
loss, so supervision is applied only to the assistant-side compact target.
Gradient checkpointing and length-bucketed batching reduce memory use. The
adapted modules are the attention projections and the feed-forward up, down,
and gate projections.

The compact target reduces output overhead. It encodes document metadata,
entity identifiers, source-copied entity strings and types, and relation
identifiers, endpoint references, types, and claim status; it does not ask the
generator to emit evidence objects or offsets. Deterministic expansion binds
the copied entity strings to supplied source segments and reconstructs global
offsets. Repeated occurrences are assigned in source order, whereas absent
strings and incomplete endpoint references are logged and omitted. Relation
evidence is initialized from a common source segment when both reconstructed
endpoints occur there and is subsequently checked by the verifier.

\subsection{Deterministic relation verification}

For every generated relation, the verifier checks: (i) both entity identifiers
exist and are different; (ii) the relation type is known; (iii) endpoint types
satisfy the ontology signature; (iv) claim status is known; (v) entity and
relation evidence are well formed; and (vi) both endpoint strings occur in at
least one local relation-evidence span. Rejected relations and all rejection
reasons are written to an immutable audit record.

Let $V(r)\in\{0,1\}$ denote this conjunction of checks. The EVGE relation set is
defined in Equation~\eqref{eq:verified-relations}:
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{EVGE}}=\{r\in R_{\mathrm{HRGE}}:V(r)=1\}.
\label{eq:verified-relations}
\end{equation}
This operation guarantees the implemented structural and local-evidence
conditions only. It neither changes the generator nor guarantees that a causal
interpretation is substantively correct.

\subsection{Conservative entity abstention}

HRGE is recall oriented and can over-generate entities. The conservative gate
retains an HRGE entity $e$ when at least one gold-independent condition in
Equation~\eqref{eq:entity-gate} holds:
\begin{equation}
A(e)=A_{\mathrm{agree}}(e)\lor A_{\mathrm{anchor}}(e)
\lor A_{\mathrm{endpoint}}(e),
\label{eq:entity-gate}
\end{equation}
where the signals indicate exact normalized text-and-type agreement between EAE
and HRGE, a type-matched source-gated HRGE anchor, or participation in an EVGE
relation, respectively. Duplicate normalized text--type candidates are merged
within a window while global offsets preserve distinct source mentions during
evaluation. A verified relation is retained only when both endpoints survive
the entity gate. Each decision records the conditions that fired.

This composition exploits recall--abstention complementarity: HRGE supplies a
broad candidate set, while independent agreement, source anchors, and verified
relation endpoints define a conservative acceptance region. No validation gold
is read by the gate.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Research questions}

\begin{description}
  \item[RQ1 -- Effectiveness.] Under strict global-character-span matching,
  how do SOE, EAE, HRGE, EVGE, CFE, and PGE compare?
  \item[RQ2 -- Constraint compliance.] How do evidence gating, deterministic
  verification, and abstention affect unsupported claims, illegal signatures,
  evidence correctness, and output failures?
  \item[RQ3 -- Bilingual behavior.] How stable are effectiveness and safety
  results across English railway reports and Chinese safety documents?
  \item[RQ4 -- Efficiency.] How do extraction and resource use
  change at 10, 25, 50, and 100 reviewed training documents?
\end{description}

\subsection{Low-resource protocol}

The document budgets are nested exactly as
$D_{10}\subset D_{25}\subset D_{50}\subset D_{100}$. Document and duplicate
clusters are indivisible, and selection balances language and source strata
without using validation performance. Each trainable system is run with seeds
20260830, 20260831, and 20260901, yielding 36 training runs. Derived verification
and gating require no additional fitting. Every output directory is immutable
and records input hashes, implementation hashes, environment versions, failure
state, and telemetry.

document-level primary table.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics}

The primary evaluator first resolves every window prediction to global
character offsets and merges all overlapping and rescue-window predictions by
document. Entities are de-duplicated by document, span, and type; relations are
de-duplicated by document, typed directed endpoints, and relation type. It then
performs one-to-one matching against the 1,001 unique validation entities and
343 unique validation relations. A secondary relation score additionally
requires claim status.
Micro-averaged precision, recall, and F1 are defined in
Equation~\eqref{eq:prf-metrics}:
\begin{equation}
P=\frac{TP}{TP+FP},\qquad
R=\frac{TP}{TP+FN},\qquad
F_1=\frac{2PR}{P+R}.
\label{eq:prf-metrics}
\end{equation}
English and Chinese results are separately micro-aggregated. Each distinct
global source span remains a separate mention, whereas repeated appearances of
that same span in overlapping windows receive only one vote. Normalized-text
and window-level metrics are diagnostic only because they can collapse
repeated mentions or overweight source regions covered by multiple windows.

Constraint-compliance metrics include entity and relation evidence correctness,
unsupported-relation rate, illegal-signature rate, valid entity-reference rate,
JSON success, and verifier acceptance/rejection counts. Rates are aggregated
over predicted objects. Empty jobs do not create artificial zero-valued
evidence objects, but missing or failed jobs remain empty predictions in
effectiveness scoring.

\subsection{Statistical analysis and efficiency}

For a budget with three completed seeds, we report the mean and sample standard
deviation; a single completed seed is labeled $n=1$ and receives no dispersion
estimate. Documents, not windows, are the resampling unit. Within-seed frozen
comparisons use 20,000 paired document bootstrap resamples for HRGE versus EAE,
CFE versus HRGE, and PGE versus HRGE. Cross-seed paired differences are reported
descriptively because three seeds do not support a reliable distributional
test. A budget is never completed by selecting a favorable seed or imputing a
running pipeline.

Reported training telemetry includes wall-clock time, examples per second,
prompt and target tokens, peak allocated and reserved GPU memory, and peak
whole-GPU use. Electricity-only cost integrates GPU power at two-second
intervals using CNY 0.60/kWh; hardware amortization and labor are excluded.

\subsection{Negative ablations}
\label{sec:negative}

Negative results were retained to avoid post-hoc method selection. MBE, the
XLM-R hard-BIO comparison system, reached 8.51\% strict entity F1. A learned conditional random
field (CRF) reached only
2.86\% and left both strict typed endpoints reachable for 2 of 491 gold
relations. A frozen span-boundary model increased endpoint reachability to
25/491 but emitted 8,115 validation spans and reached only 6.28\% strict entity
F1; its difference from hard BIO was $-2.23$ points (95\% CI $-5.26$ to
$+0.36$, $p=0.08395$). The promotion gate failed, so no relation head was
trained for that model.

The BGE-M3 local-pair relation classifier reached at most 0.92\% validation
relation F1. Adding textual KG features reached 0.85\%, with no significant
gain over the no-KG variant ($p=0.7181$). A sampled character-TF--IDF logistic
verifier also failed to produce a meaningful improvement. These findings show
that relation extraction is limited by mention boundaries, typed endpoint
reachability, sparse positive pairs, and heterogeneous evidence, rather than by
a single decoder or class-weight choice.

\section{Ablation and error analysis}
\label{sec:ablation}

\subsection{Contribution of graph context and selection}

The EAE--HRGE comparison tests a context-strategy transition rather than graph
presence alone: HRGE changes anchors, edge priors, semantic patterns, and
evidence instructions. At D100 it increases mean entity and relation recall,
but also over-generates. The HRGE--CFE and HRGE--PGE comparisons isolate the
frozen gold-independent selection pipeline more directly. PGE improves entity
F1 by 2.06--3.33 points and relation F1 by 1.08--1.94 points over HRGE in the
three D100 seeds. At D10, however, the verifier abstains from every relation
that could receive strict credit. The supported conclusion is therefore that
the staged design improves D100 retained-graph precision and auditability and
enforces the stated safety checks at smaller budgets, not that every KG
component independently improves extraction in every regime.

\subsection{Observed failure modes}

The primary errors are wrong entity boundaries, wrong entity types, missing
typed relation endpoints, unsupported generated relations, illegal endpoint
signatures, and cross-language/domain confusion. Long source passages create a
second failure class when relevant endpoints occur far apart; relation-rescue
windows address coverage but can remove broader discourse context. Chinese
administrative templates also generate repeated generic anchors that may have
high corpus support but weak instance specificity.

The current audit stratifies failures by entity type, relation type, language,
source domain, evidence distance, rescue-window status, and verifier rejection
reason. The large HRGE invalid-relation rate shows that endpoint, status, and
evidence failures coexist; it should not be interpreted as an
ontology-signature error rate alone. Representative source quotations are not
reproduced here because redistribution and privacy review remains source
specific.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What problem the framework solves}

The framework addresses a narrow but important gap between extracting many
plausible risk statements and constructing a risk graph that an analyst can
audit. A standard generator may improve recall by exploiting domain context,
yet its output can mix source facts with graph priors. Our design preserves the
distinction: the KG proposes candidates, provenance rules prevent training and
evaluation leakage, and evidence/signature checks determine whether a relation
may enter the retained graph. The result is not a complete solution to safety
reasoning, but a reproducible way to reduce unsupported graph edges while
retaining explicit evidence for human review.

\subsection{Interpretation of current evidence}

Validation results support three bounded observations. First, richer KG
context recovers more D100 candidate entities and relations, with HRGE reaching
the highest mean candidate recall. Second, conservative selection is most
useful for the retained graph: PGE improves D100 entity and relation F1 over
HRGE in every seed while reducing the measured unsupported and rule-invalid
rates to zero by construction. Third, the English/Chinese gap and the D10
relation abstentions show that a human-review workflow remains necessary. The
framework is therefore suited to prioritizing and organizing review rather
than unattended causal or regulatory decisions.

\subsection{Implications for low-resource safety analysis}

In low-resource settings, a small reviewed corpus serves two roles: it adapts
the extractor and supplies graph priors. Without provenance controls, those
roles can create hidden leakage. Recorded training loss decreases across
optimization at all four budgets, and the available D10/D25 validation groups
show that the deterministic gate continues to enforce zero measured
unsupported retained relations in those single-seed runs. These observations
do not establish cold-start effectiveness, whereas the D100 three-seed result
provides the stronger evidence that graph conditioning plus provenance gating
can assist risk-element identification, relation screening, evidence tracing,
and review prioritization.

\section{Limitations, ethics, and data availability}
\label{sec:limitations}

The benchmark is modest and combines languages, report genres, and regulatory
contexts. Its ontology simplifies contested causal interpretations into a
finite set of typed edges. Independent human agreement has not yet been
reported, and teacher-assisted pre-annotation may introduce anchoring bias even
when humans make final decisions. D10 and D25 currently have one completed
validation seed, D50 validation is not included, and the formal test remains
sealed; current validation findings may therefore not generalize. A single 4B
model family and one local GPU also limit architectural and hardware
conclusions.

Deterministic verification has an intentionally narrow guarantee. Local
co-occurrence and a legal type signature do not prove causality, compliance, or
truth. Conversely, a valid cross-sentence relation may be rejected when both
endpoints do not occur in the permitted evidence span. Abstention therefore
reduces graph coverage and cannot replace expert adjudication.

The corpus uses public safety documents, but public availability does not imply
unrestricted redistribution. Before release, each source will be checked for
license, privacy, and sensitive personal information. Where full text cannot
be redistributed, the project will release lawful metadata, hashes, source
links, schemas, split manifests, evaluation code, and annotations to the extent
permitted. Model/API provenance, prompt versions, human-review status, and
generation failures are recorded. The system must not be used to assign
individual blame or automate safety-critical decisions.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study develops a provenance-preserving, evidence-gated KG framework for
low-resource safety information extraction by composing leakage-controlled
graph priors, evidence-preserving windows, high-recall candidate generation,
conservative abstention, and deterministic relation verification. Across the
three completed D100 seeds, PGE ties CFE for the highest mean entity F1 and
achieves the highest mean relation F1 among the reported systems, and every retained relation satisfies the
implemented provenance, endpoint, ontology, claim-status, and local-evidence
checks. The available low-budget results and the complete 36-run training
record document its observed behavior across training budgets. These
properties make it suitable as an auditable assistant
for risk-element identification, relation screening, evidence tracing,
risk-graph construction, and human-review prioritization in low-resource
bilingual safety analysis.
